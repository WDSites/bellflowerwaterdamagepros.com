Seventy-Year-Old Homes: What the Lakewood Build Era Means

Lakewood is a planned community. Sales began on March 24, 1950, and about 17,500 houses went up in under three years, with one day in which 110 were completed. A house finished in 1953 is 73 years old in 2026, and original materials from that era, including drain lines and roofing, are well past a typical service life.

That does not mean every Lakewood home has a problem. It means that when a leak does start, the age of the plumbing and the layers of paint and drywall in an older wall change how a repair is scoped. The first step is always moisture readings that show how far the water went.

When Water Is Spreading in Lakewood

A burst pipe or failed appliance line in a Lakewood tract home can move water into adjoining rooms fast. Shut off the nearest valve, then the main if you cannot find the source, and call. The line is answered 24 hours a day and after-hours calls are handled the same way as daytime calls.

Same-day response is offered when crews are available. We do not promise an arrival time. Describe the rooms that are wet and the source, if you know it.

Find the shut-off

Two Water Utilities Serve Lakewood

The City of Lakewood says it is served by two water utilities, Golden State Water Company and the city's own Water Resources Department. Golden State Water serves residents east of the San Gabriel River. The city handles leak reports at (562) 866-9771, extension 2700, and after-hours emergencies through the same number, which pages on-call staff.

Golden State Water customers can call 1-800-999-4033. A leak between the meter and the house is yours to fix, and a leak on the street side belongs to the utility.
