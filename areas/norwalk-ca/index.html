Landmarks That Place a Call in Norwalk

Norwalk is the sort of city where dispatchers navigate by landmark. Cerritos College, founded in 1955, anchors the south side. The Norwalk and Santa Fe Springs Metrolink station and the Metro C Line Norwalk station sit near the I-5 and I-105 corridor, and the 162-acre Metropolitan State Hospital campus is a familiar reference point.

Older buildings add history. The Sproul House museum was built in 1870 and the Hargitt House in 1891. For a restoration call, those are references for directions, not job sites. The useful part is knowing the street, the nearest cross street and the type of building, which lets the crew arrive with the right equipment.

Four Water Providers Serve Norwalk

The City of Norwalk says its customers are served by the Norwalk Municipal Water System, Liberty Utilities, the City of Santa Fe Springs and Golden State Water. The municipal system has about 5,300 connections serving over 22,000 Norwalk and Artesia residents. To find out which one serves you, use the city's Map of Water Providers.

Reports of cloudy water, a leaking meter or low pressure go to the provider that serves the address. City Hall is at 12700 Norwalk Boulevard, Room 5, and the phone number is (562) 929-5766. Our crews handle what is inside the building.

Homeowners and Dairy Country

Norwalk had 28,455 housing units in 2020, and 64.6 percent were owner occupied. Norwalk began as a dairy center around 1900, with Dutch dairy farmers and large sugar beet farms. That is well above Bellflower's 39 percent, so homeowner policies matter more here than renters policies.

Permit rules for a rebuild vary, so ask the Norwalk building department before work starts. Insurance questions are between you and your insurer, and we supply readings, photos and a written scope.

Safe First Steps While You Wait

  • If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, stay out and leave the power off until it is checked.
  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Move people, pets and small valuables out of the wet area.
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved.
  • Call 911 first for fire, gas odor or any risk to life.

Drying plans on this site follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which sorts water into categories and sets how wet materials are handled. This describes the published industry standard and not a certification claim, and homeowners can ask any company which standard its crews work to.
