The First 48 Hours After a Leak

The first two days decide whether a wet room gets cleaned or condemned. Carpet pad, wet drywall at the floor line and soaked insulation hold water against materials that would otherwise dry. Removing them early is usually cheaper than treating the odor and mold that follow.

Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and after-hours calls are answered by the same 24/7 dispatch line. While you wait, lift valuables, open windows for air if the weather allows and keep everyone out of any water that came from a drain or outdoors.

What Cleanup Includes

Crews separate materials into three groups: what can be dried and kept, what can be cleaned and sanitized, and what must be bagged and removed. Carpet pad is nearly always removed, carpet itself is sometimes saved after cleaning, and drywall that wicked water upward is cut a few inches above the visible line.

After debris is out, hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial suitable for the water category. Odor control comes last, since a smell that survives drying usually means something wet was left behind. Cleanup can overlap with water mitigation on the same visit, and the scope keeps them distinct.

When Cleanup Is an Emergency

Contaminated water is the clearest emergency. Water from a toilet, drain or storm drain should not be handled by residents, and any room that smells of sewage needs trained crews with proper protective equipment. See flood damage restoration for backups and outdoor water.

Clean water becomes less clean the longer it sits. Water that began as a supply line leak can pick up bacteria from the carpet, the pad and the floor, which is why the first two days matter.

Shut off the source

Close the fixture valve or main so no new water arrives.

Do not use a household vacuum

A standard vacuum is not made for wet debris and can be damaged or become an electrical risk.

Set aside wet valuables

Photos, documents and small keepsakes can sometimes be saved if they are separated and dried right away.

Take photos first

Capture the damage before anything is removed so your insurer sees what was lost.

Cost Factors for Cleanup

Cleanup pricing follows the water category, the square footage involved, how much debris must be hauled, how far drying has progressed and what repairs come next. A kitchen floor with Category 1 water costs far less to clean than a ground floor affected by a drain backup.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and the dispatch line can describe the factors on a call.

Insurance in general terms

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep a list of discarded items with photos so contents are documented.
